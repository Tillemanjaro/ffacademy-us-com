Welcome

Take a breath. You've got this.

Minecraft can feel overwhelming at first. It isn't. By the end of this module you'll be able to do the four things that keep every new player alive.

New words we'll use (don't worry, we explain each one):
  • Block — everything in the world is a cube you can break or place.
  • Craft — combine items to make new ones (like a recipe).
  • Mob — a creature. Some are friendly, some are not.
  • Spawn — when something appears in the world.

The most important rule: watch the sun

A full day in Minecraft lasts about 20 real-world minutes. When the sun sets, dangerous creatures appear in the dark. Watch this short loop of a day turning into night:

☀️ Day → 🌙 Night → ☀️ Day (sped up)
Remember: Monsters come out at night. Your #1 job on day one is to have a shelter before it gets dark. Everything else in this module builds toward that.

Why wood before stone? Your bare hands can't break stone fast enough to be useful, and they can't collect it at all — it just crumbles. You need a wooden pickaxe first, and you use that to mine stone, which makes a stronger stone pickaxe. Tools climb a ladder: wood → stone → iron → diamond.
Lesson 4 · Build it

Build a shelter before dark

You don't need a mansion. A simple 5-block-wide box with a door and a torch will keep you completely safe. Click the empty squares to place walls before the sun sets.

☀️ Daytime left100%
Real-game shortcut: Caught out with night falling and no walls? Dig 3 blocks straight down, then place a block on top of your head to seal yourself in. Instant emergency bunker. (Never dig straight down in a real deep cave, though — you can drop into lava.)
Lesson 5 · Simulation

Surviving the first night

Sometimes a zombie finds you before your walls are up. Don't panic — you have a stone sword. Practice the rhythm below: attack, wait for your swing to reset, attack again. Spamming the button does not hit harder.

❤️ Your health
🧟 Zombie health
A zombie is shuffling toward you. Wait for it to get close, then attack.
The pro trick: Waiting for the sword to fully reset (the button re-fills) does much more damage than clicking wildly. In the real game this is called a "critical hit" if you also tap while jumping. Rhythm beats panic.

🧠 Designer notes & recommendations

A few L&D calls I made, plus how to extend this:

  • One objective per lesson, one interaction per objective. Each of the four terminal objectives has a matching "do it" moment (hotspot, craft sim, build, combat) so the assessment is authentic, not just recall.
  • Cognitive-load choices for the audience: adjustable text size, a running jargon glossary, no real-time pressure except one optional build timer, and plain-language framing ("box," not "structure").
  • The recreated cave asset stands in for your screenshot so the file is fully self-contained. Swap in the real PNG via the note in Lesson 2 — hotspot coordinates are data-driven in the HOTSPOTS array.
  • To make this a real SCORM package (your scorm-generator.html world): wrap completion in SCORM 1.2 calls — set cmi.core.lesson_status="completed" when state.done fills, and push the quiz % to cmi.core.score.raw. I can wire that next.
  • Accessibility to add before shipping: keyboard focus states on hotspots (add tabindex + Enter handler), prefers-reduced-motion to pause the day/night loop, and alt text on the biome art.
  • Real media: two media-slot boxes mark exactly where to drop gameplay clips/landscapes without touching layout.
