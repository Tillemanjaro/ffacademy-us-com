What is PPR?
60-second lesson
▼

PPR stands for Points Per Reception. Every time a player catches a pass in the NFL, they get +1 bonus point in your fantasy score. This makes receivers and pass-catching running backs way more valuable.

Example: Tyreek Hill catches 12 passes for 90 yards. In standard scoring that's 9 points. In PPR it's 9 + 12 = 21 points — more than double!
✓ Takeaway: In PPR leagues, target players who catch a lot of passes — not just runners.
📅
What is a Bye Week?
45-second lesson
▼

Every NFL team gets one week off per season — called their bye week. During that week, none of their players score any fantasy points. If you start a player on bye, you get zero. This is one of the most common beginner mistakes.

Example: Josh Allen's Bills have a bye on Week 7. If you start Josh Allen that week, he plays no game and gets you 0 points — even though he's a great QB.
✓ Takeaway: Check bye weeks before setting your lineup every week. Copilot will remind you automatically.
🔄
What is FLEX?
45-second lesson
▼

The FLEX spot is a wildcard roster slot. You can put any RB, WR, or TE in it — whoever you think will score the most that week. It gives you flexibility to play matchups.

Example: You have 3 good RBs but one is hurt. Normally your third RB goes to bench. With FLEX, you can start that third RB and still have your 2 WRs in the WR slots.
✓ Takeaway: Use FLEX to start your best available RB/WR/TE that isn't already in a starter slot.
💰
What is FAAB?
60-second lesson
▼

FAAB stands for Free Agent Acquisition Budget. Instead of a priority order on waivers, everyone gets a $100 budget at the start of the season. You secretly bid on players — highest bid wins, and that money is gone for the season.

Example: Derrick Henry gets hurt and his backup is suddenly available. You bid $18. Someone else bids $25. They get the player. You keep your $18. Manage your budget all season.
✓ Takeaway: Don't blow your FAAB early on risky pickups. Save $30–40 for the second half of the season when injuries pile up.
🔀
What is Streaming?
45-second lesson
▼

Streaming means picking up a player for one week based on a good matchup, starting them, then dropping them the following week to grab a new matchup play. You're not keeping them — just renting them for one game.

Example: The Browns defense plays the Raiders this week. Raiders are terrible. You drop your current mediocre defense, add Browns DST, start them for Week 7, score big, then drop them next week when they have a harder opponent.
✓ Takeaway: Stream kickers and defenses every week. You don't need to keep these roster spots locked up.
🤝
What is a Handcuff RB?
60-second lesson
▼

A handcuff is the backup running back on the same NFL team as your starting RB. If your RB1 gets injured, the handcuff becomes the starter and is suddenly worth a lot. Owning them "handcuffs" opponents from getting them.

Example: You own Bijan Robinson (ATL). Tyler Allgeier is Atlanta's backup. If Bijan gets hurt for 3 weeks, Allgeier steps into a full workload. By already owning Allgeier, you don't lose as much production.
✓ Takeaway: Always handcuff your RB1. It's insurance for your most important player.
📊
What is ADP?
45-second lesson
▼

ADP means Average Draft Position — the pick number where a player is typically selected across thousands of drafts. If a player's ADP is 24, most people draft them around pick 24.

Example: Justin Jefferson has an ADP of 7. That means most teams take him around the 7th overall pick. If you're picking 10th and he's still available, that's excellent value — you got a top-7 player at pick 10.
✓ Takeaway: Getting players later than their ADP = good value. That's how you win drafts.
