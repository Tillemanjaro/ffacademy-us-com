Step 3 of 5

Bambu Studio (the Slicer)

A slicer is the software that takes a 3D model file and converts it into instructions your printer can follow. Think of it as the translator between design and machine.

What is slicing? Your model is a solid 3D shape. The printer can only move in layers — typically 0.1–0.3mm thick. The slicer "slices" your model into hundreds of thin horizontal layers, then tells the print head exactly where to move and when to extrude plastic on each one.
1

Download Bambu Studio

Free from bambulab.com. Available on Windows, Mac, and Linux. Install and select your printer model during setup.

2

Import your model file

Drag and drop an .STL or .3MF file onto the build plate. You'll see your 3D model appear in the workspace.

3

Pick a print profile

Bambu Studio gives you presets: 0.2mm Standard (best for most things), 0.1mm Detail (slower, sharper), 0.3mm Draft (fast, rougher). Start with 0.2mm.

4

Add supports if needed

If your model has overhangs (parts that float in mid-air), click "Support" and let Bambu auto-generate them. The slicer is smart about where they're needed.

5

Slice and send

Hit the orange "Slice" button. It takes seconds. You'll see a layer-by-layer preview and an estimated time. Click "Print" to send it to your printer over WiFi.

Key settings to know:

📏

Layer Height

Thinner = more detail, slower print. Thicker = faster, less smooth. 0.2mm is the sweet spot.

💪

Infill %

How solid the inside is. 15% for decorative pieces, 40%+ for functional/structural parts.

🐢

Supports

Temporary scaffolding that holds up overhangs during printing. Break away when done.

🔲

Bed Adhesion

Brim or raft adds a flat border around your print to stop it lifting off the plate mid-print.

Step 4 of 5

AMS — Automatic Material System

The AMS is Bambu's filament hub. It lets your printer automatically switch between multiple spools mid-print — giving you multi-color or multi-material prints with zero manual intervention.

How the AMS works

Each slot holds one spool. The printer pulls from whichever color is needed at each layer.

🔴Slot 1
Red PLA
🔵Slot 2
Blue PLA
🟢Slot 3
Green PLA
⚫Slot 4
Black PLA
↓
Your Printer 🖨️

Printer pauses, retracts old filament, loads new color, purges the mixing zone, then continues printing

🎨

A1 Mini — AMS Lite

Comes bundled with the A1 Mini Combo. Holds 4 spools. Lighter build, perfect for PLA. Can be chained (2× AMS = 8 colors).

🏭

A1 — Full AMS

The full AMS also holds 4 spools, but can be daisy-chained up to 4 units — giving you 16 colors in a single print.

🗑️

Purge Tower / Waste

Every color change needs a small purge to clear the nozzle. The slicer auto-generates a purge tower. It's a little waste — totally normal.

🧵

Stick to PLA First

PLA works in every AMS slot with no hassle. Once you're comfortable, branch into PETG, ABS, or TPU — these need more settings tweaking.

Fun first AMS project: Search MakerWorld for "flexi dragon" or "lithophane." These multi-color models will immediately show you what the AMS can do — and they look incredible as a first print.
Step 5 of 5

Design Your Own Models

Once you're printing other people's files, you'll start to wonder — "what if I made this myself?" Here are the two best tools for hobbyists, and both are free.

🧱

Tinkercad

Browser-based · Completely free · Beginner

Tinkercad is drag-and-drop CAD in your browser — no download, no account cost. You combine simple shapes (boxes, cylinders, wedges) to build more complex designs. It's genuinely fun and you can make something printable in under 10 minutes on day one.

Zero learning curve Runs in browser Exports .STL directly Great for enclosures & organizers
Best for: Beginners, kids, quick practical designs — a custom phone holder, a wall bracket, a nameplate.
Try Tinkercad Free ↗
⚙️

Fusion 360

Desktop app · Free for hobbyists · Intermediate

Fusion 360 is professional-grade CAD software used by engineers and product designers. Autodesk offers a free personal/hobbyist license for non-commercial use. It has a steeper curve than Tinkercad, but unlocks parametric design — meaning you can change one dimension and the whole model updates intelligently.

Parametric modeling Free for hobbyists Industry-standard tool Organic shapes + precise parts
Best for: Intermediate users who want to design functional, precise, or complex parts — gears, joints, custom tools.
Get Fusion 360 Free ↗

The learning path that works:

1

Print others' models for 2–4 weeks

Get comfortable with the printer, the slicer, filament, and supports before adding design to the mix.

2

Start with Tinkercad — modify an existing model

Import a model from Printables into Tinkercad, change its size or add a hole for a screw. A small tweak teaches you a lot.

3

Design something you actually need

The best motivator is a real problem. A custom bracket, a holder for your specific desk item, a clip that doesn't exist on any site.

4

Graduate to Fusion 360 when Tinkercad feels limiting

When you find yourself fighting Tinkercad's limits — snapping, precision, complex curves — that's your sign. Fusion's learning curve pays off quickly.

You're ready. Printer → models → slicer → AMS → design. That's the whole journey. The only thing left is to start. Your first print is one download away.
