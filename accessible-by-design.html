Take‑home · The secret sauce

Recipe: Accessible by Design

Five ingredients you can use this week — no budget approval required.

1 Reframe with the social model ▾

Disability isn’t a person’s “problem” to fix — it’s a mismatch between a person and an environment. Fix the environment. Every barrier shows up three ways, so you’re always designing for more people than you think:

Examples of permanent, temporary, and situational barriers
PermanentTemporarySituational
BlindnessEye surgeryGlare on a phone outside
One armBroken wristHolding a baby
DeafnessEar infectionMuted phone on the bus
2 Empathy‑map before you build ▾

Before storyboarding, map one real learner with a barrier: what do they say, think, see, hear, feel, and do while taking your course? Ten minutes of empathy mapping catches the “steps you’ve missed” while they’re still cheap to fix. Better yet — survey your people and form a small advisory board of learners with disabilities.

3 Write an accessibility mission statement ▾

One or two sentences, public, updated, with leadership buy‑in — like Barclays (“accessible by design… designing for difference means designing for everyone”) or OXO. Concise but clear, an invitation to connect, and reviewed yearly. Use AI and your brain — start with this prompt:

I work for a [your industry] company and I want to develop an accessibility mission statement. Ask me questions one at a time to make sure you understand my company, our learners, and our values — then help me draft a statement that is concise, invites people to connect, and can be shared publicly.
4 Run the UDL 3‑question check ▾

Universal Design for Learning, pocket edition. Ask of every module:

  • Engagement (the WHY): do learners have more than one reason and way to care?
  • Representation (the WHAT): is every key idea available in more than one format — text, audio, visual?
  • Expression (the HOW): can learners show understanding in more than one way?

If a step only works with perfect eyes, ears, hands, and focus — that’s a step you’ve missed.

5 Hold your vendors accountable ▾

Your LMS and authoring tools are ingredients too. Before you buy (or renew), ask three questions and expect real answers:

  • “How do you test for accessibility — with real assistive‑tech users, or just an automated checker?”
  • “What is your accessibility mission statement?”
  • “What’s on your accessibility roadmap for the next 12 months?”
